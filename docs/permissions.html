Permissions.

How contributions to our community website are reviewed, approved and updated, and how to ask about an existing photograph, profile or story.

A shared learning space

Your story. Your choice.

ARU–CJLU is a student-led learning website. Profiles, photographs and stories can help us celebrate the community, but participation must be voluntary. Saying no, asking for an edit or withdrawing permission must not affect someone's teaching, assessment or place in the community.

01

The contributor is shown the proposed text and the specific images or quotations intended for publication.

02

Choose

Approval is requested separately for names, photographs, quotes and other identifiable contributions as appropriate.

The date, wording, material approved, permission evidence and later changes are recorded in a restricted-access register.

04

Review

Contributors can request a correction or withdrawal. Requests are reviewed promptly and the record and website are updated as appropriate.

Publication scope

What approval should cover

Before an identifiable contribution is posted, the permission request should describe the precise intended use. A student profile is not automatically permission to use the same photo or quotation in a social media post.

  • Name and biography on the students or teaching-team pages.
  • Photographs including portraits, group photos and event images.
  • Quotations and personal experiences reproduced in Our Stories.
  • Project work, images, code or other student-created material, including any relevant copyright permissions.
  • Separate publicity such as external social media, if requested.

Where an event photograph shows other identifiable people, the editor should check whether the necessary permissions or another suitable basis are in place before using it.

Before you say yes

Where your material appears

This website is public. Its pages and uploaded images are stored in a public GitHub repository. Material can be viewed, copied or shared by others, and previous Git versions or external caches may retain copies after the live page is changed.

Please review what will be published and raise any concerns before giving approval. The project team should avoid uploading identifiable images while publication approval is unresolved.

Record keeping

Where are permissions kept?

The website should maintain an internal, access-restricted record of publication permissions. Each record should identify the contributor, the approved materials and page, the version of the permission request, the date and method of approval, and any later edits or withdrawal requests.

Individual signed forms, private messages and student contact details are not published here. They should be stored in an appropriately restricted institutional system, not committed to the public GitHub repository.

This page explains the intended approval and recordkeeping process; it is not a public certification that every historical publication has already been checked.

What can visitors see?

Transparency without exposing personal records

Visitors can read this publication-permissions statement and enquire about how material is used. An individual can ask to review or correct information about themselves, subject to appropriate identity checks.

We do not offer public downloads of other people's consent forms, emails or individual permission records.

Questions, changes and withdrawal

Want to check or change something?

For your own profile, photo or story, you can ask what information is published, request a correction, ask for removal, or enquire about the relevant approval record. Please include the page or item concerned. We will review the request and may need to confirm your identity before discussing personal records.

Withdrawing permission should be as easy as giving it. Removing material from the live site cannot guarantee removal of copies already shared or stored in public Git history.
